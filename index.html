The figures above are guesses. Knit a few small swatches, measure the yarn each one ate, and they become measurements instead. Say which stitches you use and how much knitting you are prepared to do, and you will be told exactly what to make.

Stitch Include Needs carrying

Needs carrying is for a stitch that cannot make a fabric on its own — slip every stitch of every row and the yarn never moves. Those are worked into a swatch alongside an ordinary stitch instead.

Measurements

A scale gives grams and the solver needs length, so the yarn has to convert one into the other. Measure a few known lengths, weigh them, and the line through those points is the conversion. Two pairs will do; more is better.

Length () Weight (g)

Both come off every measurement — they are yarn you held, not yarn the fabric ate. They are asked for separately because they rarely match: the cast-on tail is whatever you guessed at the start, the bind-off tail is whatever was left.

Row template

One entry per box. 3k means three knits. A * before a stitch opens a group and one after it closes the group, followed by how many times — so s *3k 3p*23 s is a 140 stitch row with slipped edges. Enter, space or Tab moves to the next box. The strip above counts stitches as you go; while a group is open it counts the group instead, and clears once you close it.

Rows are worked once unless you mark a repeat. Tick start on the first row of a block and end on the last, then say how many times — tick both on one row to repeat just that row. The template then states the whole fabric, so the stitch and row counts follow from it. Every row has to come out the same width.
